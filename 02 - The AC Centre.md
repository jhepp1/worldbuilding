# The AC Centre

The AC Centre is one of the most famous buildings in the city.

Outwardly, it resembles a monumental civic complex of white stone, glass
towers, bridges, landscaped courtyards, terraces, fountains, and
enormous gardens.

Citizens often call it **the Jewel**.

## Interior Scale

The Centre is much larger inside than its external footprint suggests.

Its architecture is adaptive and partially non-Euclidean.

Corridors can change.

Rooms can be rearranged.

Residential districts can expand or contract.

Routes can subtly redirect residents toward activities or familiar
areas.

The Centre contains approximately **1,800 residents**.

Most residents know only a small portion of the building.

## Fixed Anchor Locations

Some locations remain constant:

-   Main reception hall
-   Central gardens
-   Enormous glass atrium
-   Main fountain
-   Major dining halls
-   Certain medical areas

Other spaces can change.

## The Small-Town Effect

The Centre functions like a self-contained town.

It contains:

-   Cafés
-   Restaurants
-   Libraries
-   Recreation halls
-   Gardens
-   Walking routes
-   Activity rooms
-   Theatres
-   Medical facilities
-   Residential districts
-   Shops and service spaces

A resident can spend months inside without encountering most of the
population.

## Environmental Control

The building continuously monitors residents and adjusts:

-   Temperature
-   Lighting
-   Furniture
-   Routes
-   Activity schedules
-   Rest periods
-   Social opportunities
-   Accessibility
-   Environmental atmosphere

The Centre is designed so thoroughly around comfort that leaving can
gradually feel unnecessary.
